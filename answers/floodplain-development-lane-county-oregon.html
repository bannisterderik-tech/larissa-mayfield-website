Floodplain

The property is in a flood zone. What does that actually mean?

Short answer

It means development there is regulated, and that the rules come from your city or county rather than from the state. Oregon's Department of Land Conservation and Development is explicit that cities and counties adopt the floodplain maps and regulations and issue floodplain development permits locally — there is no single statewide elevation standard to quote you. In a Special Flood Hazard Area you will generally need a floodplain development permit before building, and how far above the base flood elevation your lowest floor must sit is set by the local ordinance. For anything in Lane County, get that number from Land Management directly, in writing, before you design anything.

Written by Larissa Mayfield, Principal Broker, Real Broker LLC · Oregon licence 201231874 · Sources checked 14 August 2026

Why I will not quote you an elevation figure

Local freeboard requirements are adopted locally and they change. Publishing a number that is out of date, on a page a buyer might rely on to size a foundation, would be worse than publishing nothing.

So this page tells you what to ask and who to ask. The specific requirement for your parcel comes from Lane County Land Management Division, 3050 N. Delta Highway, Eugene — phone (541) 682-4651.

The words on the map

Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA). The area FEMA maps as having a 1% annual chance of flooding — the "100-year flood", which is a probability, not a schedule. Two in ten years is entirely possible.

Base flood elevation (BFE). How high the water is expected to reach in that event. Local rules are written in terms of height above it.

Zone A versus Zone AE. Both are high-risk. The difference is study: AE zones have a determined base flood elevation, A zones do not — they were mapped without a detailed engineering study, so no BFE was established. An A zone is not lower risk than an AE zone; it is less measured. In practice that can mean you have to produce the elevation data yourself.

Floodway. The channel and the adjoining land needed to carry the flood. This is the most restricted designation and the one most likely to make a building site unusable.

What it does to the transaction

Insurance. A federally backed lender will require flood insurance on a structure in an SFHA. Get an actual quote during your inspection period — it is a permanent line in the monthly cost and it can be substantial.

An elevation certificate documents where the building actually sits relative to the BFE, and it is what an insurer prices from. If the seller has one, get it. If not, factor in obtaining one.

Permits and design. Elevation, flood-resistant construction, anchoring and where the mechanical equipment sits are all constrained. This is a cost and design conversation to have before you are committed, not after.

Around Fern Ridge and the Long Tom

West Lane County has genuine flood geography — Fern Ridge Reservoir sits on the Long Tom River, and the low ground around it and its tributaries floods.

It does not make property there a bad buy. Plenty of good land carries a flood designation on part of it, and where the mapped area falls on a parcel matters enormously — a floodway across the back pasture is a very different fact from an AE zone under the house site.
