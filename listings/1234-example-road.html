Write the real description the way Larissa talks: what the place actually is, who it suits, and what a buyer should walk in knowing. Three or four paragraphs. Lead with the thing that makes it different, not with “welcome home.”

For rural parcels, say the quiet part out loud in the copy — the well’s output, the septic’s permit, the zoning minimum, what the outbuildings are actually good for. That is the information buyers are hunting for and almost no listing page gives them.

Land, Water & Zoning

What actually governs this parcel.

Acreage is bought and sold on infrastructure, not adjectives. Here is what governs this parcel.

Water
Private well

Well log is in the documents below. A flow test is worth ordering during inspection — it is the single most expensive thing to get wrong on a rural buy.

Septic
Standard system, permitted

County permit on file. Budget for a pump-and-inspect; Lane County records tell you the tank size and the drainfield location.

Zoning
RR5, 2-acre minimum

At 3.67 acres this parcel exceeds the minimum, which is what opens the door to an additional dwelling permit. Confirm with Lane County Land Management before you count on it.

Access
County-maintained road frontage

No shared-road maintenance agreement to negotiate.

Outbuildings
Lean-to barn, two stalls

Suits horses, 4-H stock, or storage. Not a permitted dwelling.

Financing note
Conventional, VA and USDA may all fit

Acreage and outbuilding value can affect appraisal — worth a conversation with your lender before you write.

Rural due diligence is its own discipline. If any of this is unfamiliar, read the Rural Buyer Playbook or just call me — 541.784.7745.
